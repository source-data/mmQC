# <short title>

Exploration: work done to understand, tweak, optimize or de-risk. No
hypothesis was committed in advance, so nothing here is confirmatory — which
is a description, not a caveat. Most understanding arrives this way.

## Question

What we are trying to understand. Open-ended is fine and expected; if it is
sharp enough to be wrong about, consider whether it wants to be an
experiment instead.

## What was done

Conditions tried, what varied, what was held steady. Enough that someone
could repeat it — including the things varied opportunistically mid-probe,
which is normal here and would not be in an experiment.

Note anything varied that an experiment would hold fixed (contracts,
examples, model), because it bears on what this can support.

## What happened

Numbers and observations, kept apart from their reading.

## What it does and does not establish

The important section. Say plainly what this rules in, what it rules out,
and what it merely suggests. An exploration cannot support a paper claim on
its own: a result seen without a prior prediction is a finding, and becoming
a claim requires preregistering it and testing again.

## Open questions

What this opened up. Anything sharp enough to predict belongs in an
experiment — link it here once preregistered, so the lineage from
exploration to claim stays visible.

## Cost

Runs, model, rough spend. Useful when deciding whether a follow-up is worth
it.
